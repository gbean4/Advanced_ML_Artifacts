Sparse SVM: Regularization Geometry

How to Use This Artifact

Toggle between the L1 Penalty and L2 Penalty to observe the constraint regions in the primal weight space ($w_1, w_2$). Adjust the Constraint Budget (C) slider to expand or shrink the allowed parameter space.

  • Red/Blue Region: The geometric constraint imposed by the regularization term (Diamond for L1, Circle for L2).
  • Green Ellipses: The empirical loss contours. The center is the unregularized optimal weight vector.
  • Black Dot: The optimized weight vector $w^*$. Notice where the lowest possible loss contour intersects the constraint region.

Why This is Helpful

This visualization bridges the gap between the algebraic KKT conditions and geometric intuition. It visually proves why $L_1$ regularization forces primal sparsity (feature selection):

Because the $L_1$ hyper-diamond has sharp vertices resting exactly on the axes, an expanding elliptical loss contour will almost always intersect it at a corner. At this corner, $w_2 = 0$, meaning Feature 2 is completely removed from the model. The $L_2$ hypersphere has no sharp corners, so the tangent point usually falls somewhere off-axis, keeping all features active with small, non-zero weights.

Study Summary: SVM & Sparsity

1. Core Distinctions
  • Standard SVM: Maximizes the margin. Achieves dual sparsity (ignores most data points; retains support vectors). Uses $L_2$ norm.
  • Sparse SVM: Focuses on feature selection. Achieves primal sparsity (ignores irrelevant features; weights go to exactly 0). Uses $L_1$ norm.
2. Textbook & Literature Extensions
  • L2VM vs L1VM: Parametric models using kernel basis functions. L2VM yields dense weights dependent on all $N$ exemplars. L1VM promotes sparsity but sometimes isn't sparse enough.
  • Relevance Vector Machines (RVM): A Bayesian alternative using Automatic Relevancy Determination (ARD). It yields the highest sparsity, provides calibrated probabilistic outputs, and doesn't require Mercer (positive-definite) kernels.
  • Tradeoffs: L1VM, RVM, and SVMs are fast at test time due to sparsity. However, L1VM and SVMs require cross-validation (slower), while RVMs can use gradient-based marginal likelihood optimization (though the RVM objective is non-convex).
3. Exam / Conceptual Prep
  • Geometry: $L_1$ forms a convex polytope (diamond); tangency occurs at vertices (axes). $L_2$ forms a smooth hypersphere.
  • KKT Proofs: In standard SVMs, complementary slackness $\alpha_i [y_i(...) - 1 + \xi_i] = 0$ forces multipliers to zero for non-margin points (Dual Space).
  • Kernel Trick Limits: $L_1$ is non-differentiable at zero, preventing a clean dual formulation dependent solely on dot products, making kernelized L1 computationally difficult compared to standard $L_2$ kernel SVMs.
