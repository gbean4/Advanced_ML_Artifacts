The High-Level Differences

Both algorithms operate by sequentially training weak learners (usually shallow decision trees) on the mistakes of the previous trees. The fundamental difference is how they penalize those mistakes, which can be understood through the analogy of two different teaching styles.

AdaBoost The Strict Teacher

  • Highly sensitive to outliers. If it finds a noisy/mislabeled data point (a "trick question"), it will exponentially increase its weight until the model forces itself to memorize it.
  • Fast, closed-form updates. Because of its specific loss function, the weights for the next tree can be calculated in a simple, elegant step without complex optimization algorithms.

LogitBoost The Pragmatic Teacher

  • Robust against noise. Evaluates on a curve. If a data point is absurdly difficult (a massive outlier), it accepts the loss and moves on, rather than warping the entire model.
  • Newton-Raphson optimization. Requires a second-order Taylor expansion to iteratively solve for the next tree, fitting continuous pseudo-residuals instead of simple classes.

Mathematical Formulation

1. Forward Stagewise Additive Modeling (FSAM)

Both algorithms build an additive function $F(x)$ by adding weak learners $h_m(x)$ with weights $\alpha_m$. Positive margins $y_i F(x_i)$ indicate correct classifications.

$$ F_m(x) = F_{m-1}(x) + \alpha_m h_m(x) $$

2a. AdaBoost (Exponential Loss)

Minimizes the exponential loss function, which causes rapid weight explosion for deeply misclassified points (negative margin).

$$ L(y, F(x)) = e^{-y F(x)} $$
Weight Update:
The weight of the $i$-th observation at step $m$ becomes:
$$ w_i^{(m)} = e^{-y_i F_{m-1}(x_i)} $$

2b. LogitBoost (Logistic Loss)

Minimizes logistic loss (binomial deviance). Approaches a linear asymptote for negative margins.

$$ L(y, F(x)) = \log(1 + e^{-2 y F(x)}) $$
Newton Step Updates:
With probabilities $p(x_i) = \frac{e^{F(x_i)}}{e^{F(x_i)} + e^{-F(x_i)}}$:
Responses ($z_i$): $$ z_i = \frac{y_i^* - p(x_i)}{p(x_i)(1 - p(x_i))} $$ Weights ($w_i$): $$ w_i = p(x_i)(1 - p(x_i)) $$
