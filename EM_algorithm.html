Understanding the Expectation-Maximization (EM) Algorithm

An interactive exploration of bound optimization in Gaussian Mixture Models.

Three Levels of Understanding

1. The Intuitive Concept (Limited Technical)

Imagine sorting a crowd of students into two high schools, but no one is wearing school colors. You don't know who goes where, and you don't know the exact profile of either school. This is a "chicken-and-egg" problem. The EM algorithm solves this by taking a random guess at the school profiles, assigning students to the school they best match, and then using that newly sorted group to calculate a better, more accurate profile for the schools. It repeats this until the groups stop changing.

2. The Algorithmic Structure (Intermediate)

Computationally, EM alternates between two specific steps to find the best-fitting parameters for a model with hidden (latent) variables:

  • E-step (Expectation): Calculates a "soft probability" (or responsibility). Instead of a hard assignment, it determines the probability that a data point belongs to a specific cluster based on the current parameter estimates.
  • M-step (Maximization): Uses the probabilistic fractional assignments from the E-step as weights to recalculate and update the mean, variance, and weight of the clusters.

3. The Mathematical Framework (ML Undergrad)

EM is a specialized form of Minorize-Maximization (bound optimization). Because maximizing the marginal log-likelihood directly is intractable for latent variable models, EM constructs a tight lower bound at the current parameter estimate and maximizes that surrogate function instead.

The surrogate function is the expected value of the complete-data log-likelihood. In the E-step, we compute the posterior distribution of the latent variables given the current parameters. In the M-step, we maximize the expected log-likelihood with respect to the parameters, which simplifies into a weighted Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) problem with closed-form solutions for models like GMMs.
