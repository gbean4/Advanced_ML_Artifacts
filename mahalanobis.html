Why Shape Matters

Unlike Euclidean distance, which treats all dimensions equally and assumes data spreads out in a perfect circle, Mahalanobis distance measures how far a point is from the center of a distribution by scaling it according to the variance and covariance of the data.

It measures distance relative to the shape of the data. A point that sits far away on the major axis of a distribution might have a small Mahalanobis distance (it follows the expected trend), while a point that is physically closer to the center but sits off-axis has a massive Mahalanobis distance (it is a statistical anomaly).
