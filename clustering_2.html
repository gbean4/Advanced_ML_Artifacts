Part B · Conversation Summary & Final Exam Reference

Complete Concept Summary & Formula Cheat Sheet

1 Context: Why Move Beyond K-Means? (Fig. 21.13)

Both mixture models and spectral clustering address the rigid limitations of standard K-Means: K-Means assumes every cluster is an equal-sized sphere in continuous space $\mathbb{R}^D$, gives only hard 0/1 assignments, and cannot handle discrete data or non-convex shapes.

Interpreting Silhouette Diagrams (Fig. 21.13): Used to pick the number of clusters $K$ in K-Means.
  • Colored block thickness (y-axis): Number of points in each cluster.
  • Horizontal axis $[-1, 1]$: Silhouette score—how close each point is to its own cluster vs. the nearest neighboring cluster (higher is better; negative values indicate likely misassignment).
  • Red dashed vertical line: Average silhouette score across all points (in Fig. 21.13, $k=3$ scores highest at $0.52$).

2 Section 21.4: Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs)

A GMM models data as a weighted sum of $K$ overlapping Gaussian distributions, allowing stretched, tilted, and overlapping clusters (and non-Gaussian mixtures can handle discrete data).

GMM Density (Eq. 21.22)
$$p(\mathbf{x}|\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k)$$

$\pi_k$: mixing weight ($\sum \pi_k = 1$), $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k$: mean, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k$: covariance.

Responsibility / Soft (Eq. 21.23)
$$r_{nk} \triangleq p(z_n = k | \mathbf{x}_n, \boldsymbol{\theta}) = \frac{\pi_k \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_n|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k)}{\sum_{k'=1}^K \pi_{k'} \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_n|\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k'},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k'})}$$

Posterior membership probability via Bayes' rule.

Hard Assignment (Eq. 21.24)
$$\hat{z}_n = \arg\max_k r_{nk}$$

Equivalently $\arg\max_k [\log p(\mathbf{x}_n|z_n=k,\boldsymbol{\theta}) + \log \pi_k]$.

Covariance Shapes (Fig. 21.14):
  • Full (a–b): Each cluster has its own $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k$ (handles tilted cigars + round blobs).
  • Tied (c–d): All clusters share one $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ (tilts every cluster the same way).
  • Diagonal (e–f): Off-diagonals are $0$; axis-aligned ellipses only (fails on tilted cigars).
  • Spherical (g–h): $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k = \sigma_k^2 \mathbf{I}$; perfect circles only (fails on tilted cigars).
Label Switching & Non-Identifiability (Sec. 21.4.1.2):

Permuting the cluster indices ($1 \leftrightarrow 2$) leaves the likelihood $p(\mathbf{x}|\boldsymbol{\theta})$ completely unchanged. This means there are $K!$ equivalent parameter solutions—a phenomenon called non-identifiability (harmless for clustering, but problematic when averaging MCMC samples across runs).

3 Training GMMs: Expectation-Maximization (EM)

Direct MLE fails to yield a closed-form solution because the observed log-likelihood has a sum inside the logarithm, coupling all parameters together:

$$\ell(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \sum_{n=1}^N \log \left( \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_n | \boldsymbol{\mu}_k, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k) \right)$$

EM introduces latent assignments $z_n$, forms the complete-data log-likelihood $\ell_c(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \sum_{n,k} \mathbb{I}(z_n=k)[\log\pi_k + \log\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_n|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k)]$, and alternates between computing its expectation $Q(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}) = \mathbb{E}[\ell_c(\boldsymbol{\theta})]$ and maximizing $Q$:

E-Step (Expectation)

Fix $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}$ and compute soft responsibilities $r_{nk} = p(z_n=k|\mathbf{x}_n,\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})$:

$$Q(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}) = \sum_{n=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K r_{nk} \left[ \log\pi_k + \log\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_n|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k) \right]$$
M-Step (Maximization — Weighted MLE)

Let $N_k = \sum_{n=1}^N r_{nk}$ be the effective cluster size:

$$\pi_k = \frac{N_k}{N}, \quad \boldsymbol{\mu}_k = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{n=1}^N r_{nk}\mathbf{x}_n$$ $$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{n=1}^N r_{nk}(\mathbf{x}_n - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k)(\mathbf{x}_n - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k)^\top$$
Monotonicity & Local Optima By Jensen's inequality, $\ell(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+1)}) \ge \ell(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})$ every iteration. Because $\ell(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ is non-convex, EM converges to a local optimum—initialization matters.
Singularity Collapse If $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k = \mathbf{x}_n$ and $\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k) \to 0$, density spikes to $+\infty$ ($\ell \to +\infty$). Prevented by diagonal regularization ($\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k + \epsilon\mathbf{I}$) or MAP estimation.
Exact Reduction to K-Means Fix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k = \mathbf{I}$, $\pi_k = 1/K$, and replace soft $r_{nk}$ with hard $r_{nk} = \mathbb{I}(k = \arg\max_{k'} r_{nk'})$. The M-step mean update becomes exact K-Means!

4 Section 21.5: Spectral Clustering & Normalized Cuts

Instead of fitting geometric shapes, spectral clustering builds a sparse weighted similarity graph $\mathbf{W}$ (connecting each node to its $k$-nearest neighbors) and partitions the graph by minimizing the cost of cutting edges between clusters.

Raw Cut (Eq. 21.28) & The Outlier Trap
$$\text{cut}(S_1, \dots, S_K) \triangleq \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^K W(S_k, \bar{S}_k)$$

Problem: Minimizing raw cut cheats by snipping off a single isolated outlier node (very few edges to cut!).

Normalized Cut & Graph Laplacian
$$\text{Ncut}(S_1,\dots,S_K) = \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{W(S_k, \bar{S}_k)}{\text{vol}(S_k)}, \quad \mathbf{L}_{\text{sym}} = \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}$$

Dividing by cluster volume forces balanced cuts. Exact minimization is NP-hard, so we compute the $K$ smallest eigenvectors of $\mathbf{L}_{\text{sym}}$ as new features and run K-Means on them.

5 Quick-Reference Comparison: GMMs vs. Spectral Clustering

Dimension Mixture Models (GMMs) Spectral Clustering
Core Basis Probabilistic modeling (overlapping distributions fitted via EM). Graph theory & linear algebra (eigenvectors of Graph Laplacian).
Best Uses Elliptical/tilted overlapping clusters, density estimation, discrete data (non-Gaussian mixtures). Non-convex shapes (concentric rings, moons, spirals), image segmentation, social networks.
Key Benefits Soft probabilities (uncertainty), flexible sizes & tilts, generative (can sample new data). No geometric shape assumptions; only requires a pairwise similarity matrix $\mathbf{W}$.
Main Drawbacks Fails on non-elliptical shapes; local optima; singularity collapse / high-D overfitting. Eigendecomposition scales poorly ($O(N^3)$ dense) for huge $N$; sensitive to $k$-NN & $\sigma$.
