Semi-Supervised Learning

1. Concept Summary

Label Propagation is a semi-supervised learning technique. It is used when you have a vast amount of data, but only a small fraction of it has human-applied labels.

It relies on the Manifold Assumption: datapoints that are "similar" (close to each other in a graph) are likely to share the same label.

Instead of training a traditional model, we construct a graph based on similarity and let the known labels mathematically "flow" or propagate through the edges to the unknown nodes until equilibrium is reached.

2. The Algorithm

Setup:

  • W : Matrix of edge weights (similarity between nodes).
  • T : Transition Matrix. Row-normalized W, representing the probability of a label jumping from node j to i.
  • Y : Label Matrix. The current probability distribution of classes for each node.

The Loop (Repeat until convergence):

  1. Propagate: Y ← TY
    Every node updates its probabilities by taking a weighted average of its neighbors.
  2. Normalize:
    Ensure each row in Y sums to 1.
  3. Clamp:
    Reset the known (labeled) nodes back to their true ground-truth values (e.g., [1,0] or [0,1]).

How to use this tool:

1. Observe the Initial State: Nodes 0 and 1 are known. Nodes 2, 3, 4 are 50/50 unknown.

2. Click "Execute 1 Loop" to run through Propagate, Normalize, and Clamp steps simultaneously.

3. Watch the graph colors blend and the Y matrix update. Stop when values stabilize (Convergence).
