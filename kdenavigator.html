Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)

What is KDE? Kernel Density Estimation is a non-parametric way to estimate the probability distribution of a dataset. Unlike a histogram, which forces data points into rigid, arbitrary "buckets" (bins), KDE places a smooth mathematical function—a kernel—over every individual data point. These individual kernels are then added together to create a smooth, continuous curve that represents the true shape of the data.

The Bandwidth (h): This is the most crucial setting. It acts as a smoothing parameter, controlling how wide the individual kernels are.

  • Low Bandwidth: Narrow mounds. The curve becomes spiky and overfits to individual points.
  • High Bandwidth: Wide mounds. The curve smooths out, potentially hiding important underlying trends (underfitting).

Kernel Types Compared:
  • Gaussian: Smooth, infinite bell curve. The default in most machine learning libraries.
  • Epanechnikov: Bounded inverted parabola. Computationally efficient as it drops exactly to zero at its boundaries.
  • Tophat: Flat, rectangular block. Gives equal weight to everything within the bandwidth.
  • Linear: Sharp peak with sloping sides. Useful for distinct boundaries.
